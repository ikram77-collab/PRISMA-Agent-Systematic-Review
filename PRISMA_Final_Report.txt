# Artificial Intelligence for Diagnostic Accuracy in Medical Imaging
## Exploratory systematic review manuscript draft — NOT a validated systematic review

## Abstract
**Background:** Artificial intelligence is increasingly studied for interpreting medical images. A systematic evidence synthesis requires transparent searches, eligibility decisions, verified outcomes, and methodological appraisal.
**Objective:** To explore primary studies evaluating AI methods for diagnostic accuracy in medical imaging.
**Methods:** We combined Block 1 records with an additional Europe PMC search. A rule-based screening agent checked titles and abstracts and attempted open-access full-text retrieval. Other agents collected source excerpts and prepared QUADAS-2 appraisal worksheets. Full eligibility and risk-of-bias judgments were reserved for human reviewers.
**Results:** 88 unique records were screened; 34 were excluded at title/abstract screening; 19 full texts were retrieved; 19 were provisionally eligible; 35 remained pending; 0 were human-confirmed included. 19 preliminary evidence records and 19 QUADAS-2 worksheets were generated.
**Conclusion:** The pipeline produces a traceable exploratory draft, but no definitive claim about diagnostic effectiveness can be made without full eligibility verification, validated PICO extraction, and completed risk-of-bias appraisal.

## 1. Introduction
AI-based image analysis has been investigated across clinical imaging modalities. Reported diagnostic performance can depend on the patient population, imaging modality, reference standard, data provenance, and validation design. The aim of this educational pipeline is to organize candidate evidence for a systematic review; it does not substitute for an independent scientific appraisal.

## 2. Methods
### 2.1. Research question and eligibility
Review topic: Artificial intelligence for diagnostic accuracy in medical imaging. The intended target evidence is primary research investigating AI-enabled diagnostic imaging with diagnostic accuracy outcomes. The implemented title/abstract screening heuristics check AI, diagnostic and imaging terms and exclude clear review articles by title. Ambiguous records are not automatically included.
### 2.2. Search and data sources
Block 1 supplied the initial papers_for_screening.json records. The additional search queried Europe PMC using the following Boolean query:
(("artificial intelligence" OR "deep learning" OR "machine learning") AND (diagnos* OR detect*) AND (sensitivity OR specificity OR accuracy) AND (imaging OR radiology OR MRI OR CT OR ultrasound OR mammography)) NOT (REVIEW:Y)
The core response was requested for abstracts and article metadata. Duplicates were merged using available DOI, PMID, PMCID, or title identifiers.
### 2.3. Screening and full-text assessment
Agent 5 used rule-based title/abstract screening and requested available open-access Europe PMC full-text XML for a limited number of candidates. Documents matching topic and study indicators were labeled provisionally eligible, not finally included. Human inclusion/exclusion decisions can be supplied in human_fulltext_decisions.csv.
### 2.4. Evidence extraction
Agent 6 identified source excerpts potentially describing populations, AI index tests, reference standards, and diagnostic outcomes. Numerical mentions were extracted by regular expressions and are not validated study-level results.
### 2.5. Risk of bias
Agent 7 created QUADAS-2 worksheets for patient selection, index test, reference standard, and flow/timing. All risk-of-bias judgments remain unclear until expert review.
### 2.6. Evidence synthesis
Agent 8 organized verified provenance and provisional results into a narrative manuscript. Meta-analysis was not performed because study inclusion and accuracy estimates were not validated.

### 3.3. Methodological quality
19 QUADAS-2 worksheets were prepared. These are not finalized risk-of-bias assessments; judgments remain unclear.

## 4. Discussion
The prototype demonstrates how screening decisions, document excerpts, and quality-appraisal prompts can be exchanged between specialized agents. The current corpus is heterogeneous and the provisional evidence may include non-primary papers. Screening based on title and keywords cannot verify study design or clinical applicability. Extracted accuracy numbers have not been matched to study cohorts, reference standards, or testing conditions.
A scientifically interpretable synthesis will require independent screening, manual study confirmation, validated PICO fields, completed QUADAS-2 judgments and reconciliation of duplicate or overlapping study populations. No pooled effect size or comparative diagnostic claim is justified at this stage.

## 5. Limitations
- No independent dual-reviewer screening has been performed.
- Only available Europe PMC open-access XML is retrieved; some records remain pending.
- Study eligibility and PICO details have not been fully validated.
- QUADAS-2 worksheets provide candidate passages, not expert judgments.
- The procedure does not perform RAG-based answer generation or a formal meta-analysis.
- Retrieval counts and corpus contents may vary across different execution dates.

## 6. Conclusion
PRISMA-Agent Block 2 produced a traceable, structured exploratory review draft from its screening, extraction and appraisal outputs. The implementation demonstrates workflow integration and reproducible file exports but not a validated systematic review. Research conclusions should be postponed until eligibility, data extraction and methodological appraisal are independently confirmed.
